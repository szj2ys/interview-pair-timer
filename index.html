Both: choose one true example

Think of a problem from work, study, volunteering or a project. What did you personally do?

Choose what you are comfortable sharing. Leave out private names and confidential details.

Agree who will speak first, then start.

Make it useful for both people

Speaker: explain the setting, your own action, and the result you can support. Listener: ask one follow-up, then name a detail you understood and a point that still needs clarity.

The full 15-minute plan
  1. Both prepare — 3 minutes
  2. Person A answers — 2 minutes
  3. Person B asks a follow-up — 2 minutes
  4. Person B gives feedback — 1 minute
  5. Person B answers — 2 minutes
  6. Person A asks a follow-up — 1 minute
  7. Person A gives feedback — 1 minute
  8. Both try a clearer answer — 3 minutes

This is a flexible practice routine, not an assessment. The suggested timings total 15 active minutes; pauses and transition time are additional.

Choose a follow-up question
  • What did you do yourself?
  • Why did you choose that approach?
  • How did you check whether it helped?
  • What would you do differently next time?

If you need thinking time: “Let me think for a moment.” If the question is unclear: “Could you say that another way?”
